A strong developer portfolio is your golden ticket to landing interviews at top tech companies. But if your portfolio is riddled with common mistakes, it might actually be doing more harm than good.

After reviewing hundreds of developer portfolios, we've identified the top 5 mistakes developers make—and how you can fix them using **Portfolio.OS**.

## 1. The "Too Much Text" Trap
Recruiters don't have time to read your entire life story. If your "About Me" section is a wall of text, they will skip it. 

**The Fix:** Keep your bio punchy. Use bullet points to highlight your core skills, years of experience, and what you are passionate about building.

## 2. Broken Links and Slow Load Times
Nothing kills a recruiter's interest faster than a portfolio that takes 10 seconds to load or has broken GitHub links. If your site is broken, they will assume your code is broken too.

**The Fix:** Host your site on a modern edge network (like Vercel) and regularly check your links. Better yet, use a platform like [Portfolio.OS](/) which handles global CDN hosting and performance optimization for you automatically.

## 3. Listing 20 "Hello World" Projects
Having 20 minor tutorial projects (like a basic to-do app or a calculator) clutters your portfolio and dilutes the impact of your actual, impressive work.

**The Fix:** Quality over quantity. Showcase **2 to 3 major projects**. For each project, include:
- A brief description of the problem it solves.
- The tech stack used.
- Links to the live demo and source code.

## 4. No Clear Call to Action (CTA)
So a recruiter loved your projects... now what? If they have to hunt for your email address or LinkedIn profile, you might lose the opportunity.

**The Fix:** Have a highly visible "Contact Me" button or a dedicated contact form at the bottom of your portfolio. 

## 5. Poor Mobile Experience
Over 50% of web traffic is mobile. If an engineering manager opens your portfolio on their phone while commuting and the layout is broken, you've lost them.

**The Fix:** Ensure your portfolio is fully responsive. If CSS Media Queries aren't your strong suit, [Portfolio.OS](/) guarantees a flawless mobile experience right out of the box with zero configuration required.
